# Domains

The 8 domains for classifying settled files within an archive entity.

---

## Resolution question

**What kind of information is this?** Each domain captures one kind of information. The primary purpose at time of filing determines the domain. When ambiguous, the relationship test helps: is this about the entity itself, or about a relationship with an external entity? Relationship-centric items go to Career or Clients. Entity-centric items go to the domain matching their administrative function.

## Domain definitions

### Identity
Who this entity IS — formally established.

Contains: IDs, passports, birth/marriage certificates, citizenship docs, registrations, memberships, academic diplomas/degrees.

Does NOT contain: professional certifications (→ Career).

### Finance
Money moving.

Contains: receipts, statements, tax filings, payments, investments, banking records, loan payments, expense records.

Does NOT contain: loan agreements (→ Legal), insurance policies (→ Legal).

### Health
Body and mind — medical information.

Contains: medical records, prescriptions, test results, diagnoses, dental, vision, wellness records.

Does NOT contain: health insurance policies (→ Legal).

### Career
Professional and academic trajectory — relationship history with institutions.

Contains: resumes, employment records, employment contracts, transcripts, coursework, professional certifications, publications, work correspondence with employers/institutions.

Does NOT contain: academic diplomas/degrees (→ Identity).

Note: Career captures the entity's trajectory *through* other entities — as employee, student, or member. Items are naturally organized by institution (employer, university, program). For an organization, Career may contain employee development, training programs, or institutional memberships.

### Clients
Business and service relationships — engagement history with those served.

Contains: client contracts, proposals, service agreements, invoices issued, work logs, timesheets, reports, deliverables, client correspondence.

Does NOT contain: the entity's own registrations (→ Identity), the entity's own tax filings (→ Finance), the entity's own insurance policies (→ Legal).

Note: Clients captures the entity's trajectory *with* those it serves — as provider, consultant, or contractor. Items are naturally organized by client (organization, individual, engagement). Career and Clients are symmetric: Career is the relationship history looking "up" (who employed/educated me), Clients is the relationship history looking "out" (who I served). Both individuals and organizations may use either domain when the content fits.

### Assets
Things owned or managed — physical and digital.

Contains: property docs, vehicle docs, warranties, equipment records, digital accounts, subscriptions, backups, data exports, software licenses.

Does NOT contain: purchase receipts (→ Finance), terms/agreements (→ Legal).

### Legal
Binding instruments — the entity's own obligations and rights, not tied to a specific professional or client relationship.

Contains: insurance policies, leases, loan agreements, wills, POA, trusts, court orders, regulatory compliance, corporate governance.

Does NOT contain: payment records (→ Finance), health records (→ Health), employment contracts (→ Career), client contracts (→ Clients).

### Events
Experiential documentation — records whose primary value is documenting a personal experience.

Contains: travel itineraries, booking confirmations, event programs, conference materials, loyalty programs, trip collections, settled experiential documentation.

Does NOT contain: event receipts (→ Finance), travel visas (→ Identity), event photos (→ Library).

Note: Events is the broadest domain. When unsure whether something is Events or another domain, the test is: is this document primarily about an experience, or primarily about money/identity/health/career/property/legal obligation? The administrative function wins over the experiential association.
